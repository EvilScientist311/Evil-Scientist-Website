Hydrogen Atomic Orbitals

The Hydrogen Atom

In quantum mechanics, the electron in a hydrogen atom is described by a wavefunction, \(\psi_{nlm}(r,\theta,\phi)\), rather than a definite classical orbit around the nucleus. The squared magnitude of the wavefunction, \(|\psi_{nlm}|^2\), gives the probability density for detecting the electron at a particular position. An atomic orbital is the three-dimensional spatial structure of this probability distribution. Regions where the wavefunction is zero are called nodes.

The principal quantum number follows \(n=1,2,3,\ldots\). It determines the electron’s energy level and the overall radial extent of the orbital.

For each \(n\), the orbital quantum number follows \(l=0,1,\ldots,n-1\). It determines the magnitude of the orbital angular momentum and the general orbital shape. The values \(l=0,1,2,3\) correspond to the \(s\), \(p\), \(d\), and \(f\) orbitals.

For each \(l\), the magnetic quantum number follows \(m=-l,-l+1,\ldots,0,\ldots,l-1,l\). It determines the projection of orbital angular momentum along a chosen axis. There are \(2l+1\) allowed values of \(m\) for each value of \(l\).

Only combinations of \(n\), \(l\), and \(m\) that satisfy these rules represent valid hydrogen states.
